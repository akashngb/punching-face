# Reconstruction and contact evidence

Reviewed September 17, 2026. Implemented prototype and research methods are distinguished below.

## The supplied research

- [World Labs Atlas](https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/atlas): a world-model reference for generating spatial representations. It does not by itself supply a measured personal arm, a fitted facial anatomy model, or an impact solver. No Atlas endpoint is used here.
- [SplatFace, arXiv 2403.18784](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18784): jointly regularizes a face model and Gaussian representation, including splat-to-surface distance and surface-aware densification. This is relevant to reconstructing identity while maintaining an editable surface. The prototype has not reproduced its optimization.
- [Using Gaussian Splats to Create High-Fidelity Facial Geometry and Texture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16397): semantic alignment and surface constraints improve geometry from sparse facial views; the paper also separates appearance/lighting and develops a neural texture. Its surface constraints explain why naive Poisson on radiance-field centers is insufficient. The prototype's constrained normal-offset fit is much simpler.
- [GaussianAvatars](https://github.com/ShenhanQian/GaussianAvatars): 89,021 Gaussians downloaded from the official `media/306/point_cloud.ply`, with `flame_param.npz`. These are local coordinates attached to FLAME triangles. FLAME 2023 model assets are a separate licensed dependency. The raw PLY must not be treated as a world-space face. Repository license and Toyota restrictions must be retained for applicable uses.
- [HyperGaussians](https://github.com/gserifi/HyperGaussians): learned, conditioned Gaussian deformation components. It is not an automatic inversion of punch photographs into volumetric physical parameters. The main branch supplies a module; avatar integrations are in its other branches.
- [Humain facial rigging](https://www.humain-studios.com/facial-rigging): useful production reference for facial controls and capture. Expression controls do not replace passive impact simulation. No Humain proprietary models or tools are used.

## The runnable online asset

The official [LAM_WebRender example](https://github.com/aigc3d/LAM_WebRender/tree/main/asset/arkit) ships `p2-1.zip`, containing `skin.glb`, `offset.ply`, animation, and vertex order data. `scripts/prepare_lam.py` adds each learned offset to its matching skin position, following the official renderer's center calculation. The result has 20,018 Gaussians and is directly rendered with Spark. It is an inferred public example, not a multiview scan of the user. `public/online-face/source.json` records the archive hash and provenance.

Raw Poisson conversion of this example took approximately 0.7 seconds but visibly failed at the eyes, mouth, and hair. Runtime alone is not evidence of a usable reconstruction. The demonstration instead uses the shipped 39,904-triangle topology, Gaussian normal displacements constrained by opacity and offset confidence, 12 smoothing passes, and Gaussian color transfer. The largest fitted normal adjustment is 2.74 mm before normalization. Gaussian appearance is retained as an optional layer that follows the mesh.

The default surface now has a 2048-pixel baked radiance texture. `scripts/bake_texture.py` renders the Gaussian covariances with CPU orthographic alpha compositing from 14 directions and projects those colors into an [xatlas](https://github.com/mworchel/xatlas-python) UV atlas. Visibility, opacity, and normal weights select views; the central face prefers the neutral front projection to reduce ghosting. The bake took 20.07 seconds in the current environment. About 7.6% of covered texels had low weighted visibility and used a best-facing fallback. The first 1024-pixel bake undersampled eye-region triangles; increasing resolution visibly reduced the artifacts. These are generated render views of the same inferred avatar, not 14 independent captured photographs.

UV duplication increases rendering to 28,197 vertices while physics stays on the 20,018-vertex surface. Automated checks verify identical movement across seams during impact and consistent exported morphs. The GLB embeds the PNG and uses an unlit material to preserve baked lighting. Its first-person frontal appearance is recognizable, but geometry still flattens hair and some facial detail is imperfectly registered. This is conventional texture baking, not the supplied paper's learned, de-lit texture reconstruction.

The Lee Perry-Smith fixture is a separate mesh -> synthetic Gaussian samples -> Poisson round trip. It is retained for deterministic conversion tests, clearly labelled as synthetic, and must never be counted as a successfully reconstructed photo-trained scan.

## Contact-specific research

[Physics-Based Simulation of Contact-Induced Facial Wrinkling](https://thomaszewski.com/projects/face-wrinkling/index.html), SCA 2026, directly addresses the requested behavior: transient compression/shear, viscoelastic skin, and spatially varying attachments. The authors use solid-shell finite elements and ligament constraints. These are substantially richer than the prototype's surface springs. This is a useful target for a later material and wrinkle model.

[Learning a Generalized Physical Face Model From Data](https://cgl.ethz.ch/publications/papers/paperYan24a.php), SIGGRAPH 2024, addresses fitting reusable physical face models to new identities. [Efficient Incremental Potential Contact for Actuated Face Simulation](https://cgl.ethz.ch/publications/papers/paperYan23b.php) addresses contact/self-intersection. Neither solver is implemented here.

Current impact response modifies actual mesh vertices, applies inward compression plus tangential movement and an outward rim, propagates displacement through mesh adjacency, and uses different heuristic stiffness by region. Recoil can be independently disabled. The held-impact browser check measured approximately 24–25 mm peak local displacement, with negligible forehead motion for a lower-face hit. Release returned the displacement to near numerical zero. This is evidence of nonrigid deformation, not a claim of biomechanical accuracy or likeness validation.

## Reference collection

The four supplied images remain unchanged, including the stock watermark. Each has a local source record and qualitative observations: cheek indentation, lip shear, eyelid closure, nearby bulging, and separation of head rotation from local strain. The local detector extracted 468 landmarks from references 1–3 and rejected reference 4. Landmarks occluded by gloves are model predictions; they must not be treated as measured 3D positions.

The search script records queries, timestamps, URLs, errors, and rejected-result counts. Browser image search provided initial matching pages and a stock high-speed footage candidate. The RSS provider returned unrelated results during validation; those were filtered rather than accepted as examples. Search results are linked for review, not bulk-downloaded or silently used to train a model. No trained impact network exists in this prototype.

To fit physical response rather than just visual controls, obtain neutral and impact sequences of the same subject, camera calibration, temporal sampling, visible contact boundary, and an explicit uncertainty model. Use existing controlled footage or simulation data; no physical punch capture is required from the user.

## Personal capture path

[COLMAP/PyCOLMAP](https://colmap.github.io/pycolmap/index.html) estimates cameras from masked, rigid multiview captures. [Brush](https://github.com/ArthurBrussee/brush) trains an actual Gaussian model on the Apple GPU. [Open3D Poisson](https://www.open3d.org/docs/release/tutorial/geometry/surface_reconstruction.html) is an experimental extraction step, only suitable when samples and normals approximate a surface. A thinness check alone does not guarantee good geometry. [SuGaR](https://github.com/anttwo/SuGaR) or a comparable surface-aware training stage remains the stronger route to arbitrary captured limbs.

MediaPipe supplies hand/body landmarks and person segmentation. The arm crop is an intersection of that mask with pose-guided regions, not a perfect semantic arm segmentation. The local camera and body worker were exercised, but no complete, adequately framed arm scan was available; training and personal mesh likeness remain unverified. Monocular tracking does not directly recover the user's eye viewpoint or hidden arm surfaces. A room capture supplies background geometry and still needs viewpoint alignment.

The arm driver now combines [pose world landmarks](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker/web_js), whose origin is the hip midpoint, and [hand world landmarks](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/hand_landmarker/web_js), whose origin is the hand's geometric center. It explicitly removes those differing origins before placing the limb relative to the estimated eye midpoint. Body wrist proximity assigns each detected hand to an anatomical side. Captured upper-arm, forearm and finger lengths are retained; monocular observations supply directions and palm orientation. An inferred body frame is calibrated from shoulders and visible torso, with an upright-camera fallback when hips are hidden. This is a reconstruction assumption, not a calibrated first-person optical camera.

The old arm driver used fixed proxy shoulder/elbow positions and only segment swing. The new driver applies palm roll to forearm and wrist rotations, and hides the captured mesh when the required landmarks are stale, absent, or unreliable. Pose and hand inference run on the same frame when a personal arm is loaded. Tests exercise twist with fixed endpoints, joint-length preservation, left/right assignment with crossed hands, skinning, and reacquisition without a phantom hit. These establish retargeting invariants, not anatomical rig quality or personal likeness. A closed-fist Poisson reconstruction can join touching fingers, so finger articulation still requires inspection of real reconstructed geometry.

## First personal arm attempt

The user captured 59 right-arm views in local job `879025fd79a341ea8b282c105f4708ee`. Camera reconstruction found a largest component of 24 images, 427 sparse points, mean reprojection error 0.922 pixels, and only 11.31 degrees of estimated camera-direction spread around the sparse points. It failed the existing registration requirement and did not start Gaussian training. This demonstrates why a low image reprojection error alone is insufficient: most views did not register and the recovered coverage was narrow.

Visual inspection found the fist beside/overlapping the face, sleeve folds, changes of wrist/arm pose, and face/torso pixels inside the coarse crop. A local [TensorFlow BodyPix](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tensorflow/tfjs-models/master/body-segmentation/src/body_pix/README.md) pilot intersected semantic arm labels with the selected pose crop. It removed contaminating face/torso pixels but also removed valid parts of the fist and elbow, so the pilot failed visual review. No geometry was invented to replace those missing regions. Originals, original masks, camera reconstructions, and the three segmentation pilot outputs are retained separately.

Capture feedback now uses a rigid arm frame to estimate viewpoint change and rejects excessive elbow/wrist articulation. A rotating wrist no longer counts as new arm-view coverage. Finished captures save locally before training; side changes and reloads restore saved metadata. These changes address observed failures, but a successful personal splat-to-mesh reconstruction remains unproven.
