# Head reconstruction: evidence, ownership and validation

Updated 19 September 2026. This is an implementation and research record, not a claim of hyperrealistic reconstruction from every video. See [OPEN_SOURCE_STACK.md](OPEN_SOURCE_STACK.md) for this machine's dependency and licensing constraints.

## Why the defects occurred

The original pipeline fitted the facial landmarks but retained generic template ears. Projecting a side photograph onto a misplaced ear also projected the real ear onto the adjacent skull. That created two apparent ears. Eyeglasses had the same ownership problem: a separate 3D accessory was added, but imprecise masks and weak side-view samples left parts of the photographed frames on the skin. Broad missing-hair material could also cover ears. Ordinary photographs were projected onto the artificial neck closure.

A second source of error was geometric: `photo_cameras.recover()` could reuse legacy self-calibrated cameras before considering a supplied field of view. Low reprojection error alone does not establish correct focal length or head depth.

Texture transitions also had independent implementation defects. A binary cleanup mask excluded the original camera even at the feather's outer edge; an observed/estimated color threshold switched abruptly between sources; and a mixed ear/skull triangle inherited its first vertex's surface label. The bake now retains feathered camera contributions, blends estimated color continuously, and determines surface ownership per texel using barycentric weights. Cyclically reordering triangle vertices no longer changes that ownership. Cleanup cache validation checks the current annotation-derived crop and mask as well as the source image, and can re-register an existing generated edit without requesting another one.

A traced bright patch at the upper ear had no usable camera sample and inherited a constant cheek-colored fallback. Unsupported ear material now continues nearby photographed color on that ear, within a bounded distance and with compatible surface normals. The opposite ear, scalp and opposite-facing folds cannot donate color. This remains estimated appearance and does not increase measured coverage.

The hair silhouette fit also treated lateral ear vertices as part of its adjustable envelope. It now pins the template ear regions and fades deformation over a 12 mm geodesic attachment margin. A separate ear-fit issue was an unnecessarily coarse step search: one nearly failing triangle reduced the entire deformation from 100% to 75%. Six bounded refinement steps now recover the largest tested safe step without relaxing the orientation, area or intersection limits.

The original landmark test checked the triangulated control cage, which is not the rendered skin. Forty-seven semantic locations are now bound barycentrically to actual surface triangles. A nested split of the geometry-training frames selects the template regularization; reserved outer views do not select that parameter. Candidates must also pass displacement, triangle orientation, area and intersection limits. After the hair, eye-depth and ear stages, the final rendered surface must pass the reserved-view projection check. Those views may still supply texture and completion evidence, so this is not a fully unseen-image appearance benchmark.

A later temple trace found that a verified but dark frontal cleanup reference could override brighter side-view skin. Cleanup now estimates a bounded, smooth color offset from the outer feather in surface space. Nearby compatible normals supply the offset; hidden frame centers, other surface parts and central frontal features do not determine that correction. This reduces exposure seams without treating synthesized skin as measured appearance.

A traced underside failure came from nearest-color continuation itself: its changing donor neighborhoods created angular wedges, and a forward blending gate left some unseen texels gray. Lower-head completion now accumulates reliable photographic colors onto their actual mesh vertices, holds those anchors fixed, solves a positive-weight harmonic field over connected surface edges, and interpolates it into the atlas using the same barycentric bindings as rasterization. Artificial neck-cap edges are excluded, UV seams do not create discontinuities, and disconnected regions without photographic anchors stay unresolved. A later nearest-face fallback runs before this continuation so it cannot overwrite the repaired chin. Confidence, mouth, ear, eye and scalp masks preserve their separate materials. This does not invent measured underside coverage.

Generated eyes now receive bounded short-range visibility shading from the fitted globe, face and lids, applied in linear light. Only generated eye parts are changed; photographic iris materials and skin remain exact. This softens the flat globe appearance but is estimated occlusion, not recovered illumination or verified eye reflectance.

A further jaw trace separated camera support from exposure preference. A grazing frontal sample with facing cosine 0.503 had physical weight 0.0041, but its 31-fold preference outweighed a clear side view with physical weight 0.6804 after the side was suppressed to 3%. The bake now fades frontal preference with the fitted surface angle below the measured outer lower lip, reaching full release toward the chin. Registered eyes, brows, nose and lips retain coherent frontal ownership. Maximum support and coverage counters no longer include exposure preference. Those counters describe projection support on prepared images, which can contain generated cleanup; they are not proof that hidden skin was photographed.

Cheek exposure sampling now rejects transparent background pixels and requires substantial support in at least two cheek patches. It retains valid bright and dark photographed colors. In particular, warm skin with red values 250–252 must not be mistaken for clipped white highlights: excluding those pixels darkened the reference in the test capture. If the frontal reference lacks enough valid cheek pixels, the closest valid frontal view supplies the color reference; the selected filename is recorded.

The later jaw/neck trace separated two additional artifacts. General missing-head color and lower-skin continuation both faded through the same uncertain texels, producing an extra bright band at intermediate confidence. Resolved lower skin now blends once from its pre-completion appearance; unresolved regions, the neck cap, mouth and scalp retain their own fallback. That pre-completion appearance can contain registered cleanup estimates and must not be described as purely measured skin.

The prominent side-jaw stripe was different: neighboring points with strong photographic support abruptly preferred differently lit cameras as their fitted normals changed. A bounded lighting-field experiment reduced held-out camera disagreement but darkened the rendered side/neck too much, so it was rejected. The implemented correction instead smooths normalized camera ownership over actual surface edges, then applies only the interpolated change to the original texel weights. It preserves rejected-view masks, retreats if masking removes too much probability, and changes only the low-frequency blend. Central features, cleanup regions, separate parts and the shared scalp classification are protected. Physical support, fallback thresholds and the original fine-detail winner stay unchanged. A second projection visits only changed texels; no camera RGB gain is fitted. The current settings use six positive half-steps with a 4 mm edge scale at the estimated model scale; this is a decaying local influence, not a hard 4 mm radius or calibrated physical measurement.

## Current pipeline

1. Preserve the imported video and timestamped segmented captures. Recover higher resolution detail from nearby native video timestamps only after matching it to the registered frame. Retain browser-decoded low-frequency color to avoid changing skin tone through decoder/HDR differences.
2. Recover overlapping camera poses. Honor supplied intrinsics, including when caches already exist. Reserve facial views to check both triangulated landmarks and semantic locations on the final rendered skin. Select identity-fit regularization using a separate inner split and reject unsafe deformations. Absolute physical scale remains estimated.
3. Measure foreground sharpness and clipped exposure at a normalized crop size. Select clear frames near the desired camera angles rather than choosing by angle alone. Keep semantically annotated reference views.
4. Ask **gpt-6-astra** to annotate actual visible structures: hair masks/flow, opaque glasses ribbons, ear outlines and ear landmarks. These are fallible observations, not ground truth. Coordinates are tied to the exact image crop and native resolution.
5. Triangulate each ear landmark from independent rays with a baseline requirement, robust outlier rejection and a maximum reprojection error of 5 registered-image pixels. After the hair envelope and head prior are fitted, fit a bounded local transform to the whole ear, then regularize it with as-rigid-as-possible deformation while pinning the facial cage, observed face and distant surface. A geodesic attachment region lets the surrounding skull adapt; side-hair bindings are rebuilt afterward. Back off if triangle orientation, area or non-adjacent crossing checks fail. Refine ear ownership against visible photographed outlines and register ear-only texture samples to the fitted surface. Retain template inner-ear folds where the photographs do not establish detail.
6. Fit the visible hair envelope from silhouettes with a post-fit crown-height bound, pinned ear regions and a smooth attachment margin. Trace exterior locks along fused photographic orientation fields. Bind roots and curve stations to the surface so facial edits cannot detach strands. Individual fibers and unseen roots remain estimates.
7. Bake one coherent atlas. A photographed ear may only color its own ear surface; locally fill its former projection on the skull and label that appearance estimated. Preserve separate eyeball material. Clean opaque accessory ribbons before projecting skin. With AI completion enabled, request glasses-free estimates for up to three source angles, register them to unchanged source features, color-match them and feather only the affected region. Reject misaligned edits. Accepted cleanup references own those visible surface regions so competing views cannot reintroduce tinted lenses or rims. Restore enclosed segmentation holes only inside verified cleanup regions; retain background connected to the silhouette. Use strong, visible photographed hair evidence to constrain fallback material; an occluding ear is not evidence of bare scalp. Continue photographic color over the connected lower-head mesh into low-confidence regions, and give the closed neck section an explicitly estimated skin material.
8. Add separate polished acetate frames, clear lens surfaces and metal hinges. Their broad reflection field is independent of the captured skin lighting. The lenses do not refract already lens-distorted photographic eyes a second time.
9. Validate topology, finite values, facial invariance and matching geometry/texture hashes before publication. Full builds and existing-capture refinement write into separate staging directories. Mesh, atlas, textures, cage, bindings and geometric audit files form an immutable bundle; one atomic release pointer makes it current. Asset requests, Newton sessions and saved editable sessions pin that release. Failed work leaves the accepted bundle available, and workers are joined before failure is reported. Previous releases remain readable for saved sessions and in-flight requests. Full builds also retain a capture-specific pre-ear surface with its topology and regularization, so subsequent detail rebuilds do not reuse a baseline from an incompatible template fit. Inspect front, both profiles, back and bottom, with glasses hidden as well as visible, then exercise deformation and export.

`head-semantics.json`, `ear-measurements.json`, `frame-evidence.json`, `eyewear-mask-audit.json` and the mesh statistics retain the evidence. Missing or inconsistent observations should stay labeled estimated or cause a quality failure; they must not silently become measured anatomy.

## What the research actually supports

**Poisson surface reconstruction** is probably the method meant by “Poisson redistribution.” It reconstructs a surface from oriented 3D points. It does not establish camera calibration, recover depth from images, remove glasses, disentangle lighting or create a facial rig. It can help after reliable depth/normal estimation. Feeding Gaussian centers into it here would confuse volumetric blobs with surface samples. [Original authors' project](https://hhoppe.com/proj/poissonrecon/), [screened Poisson with envelope constraints](https://gfx.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Kazhdan_2020_PSR/index.php).

**TRELLIS.2** uses sparse volumetric O-Voxel representations and generative models to produce geometry and material attributes. A useful design lesson is to keep geometry and appearance in a consistent 3D representation, including difficult thin structures. Generation is a prior; it does not prove that a synthesized hidden ear or hairstyle is this person's. The official implementation requires Linux/NVIDIA CUDA and associated extensions, so it does not fit this Mac's native toolchain. We have not installed it or benchmarked it against this capture. [Official repository](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14692).

**Meshy** publicly documents multiple images of the same object and PBR texture output. Those are product capabilities, not a published description of its internal training or reconstruction architecture. The transferable lesson is consistent multi-angle inputs and separate material attributes. There is no evidence here that our system exceeds Meshy, and no Meshy API request has been made. [Official multi-image API](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/api/multi-image-to-3d).

**GaussianAvatars** is more directly relevant to a deformable head: it binds appearance primitives to mesh triangles and jointly optimizes avatar geometry and appearance. The principle supports retaining a coherent surface coordinate system through facial animation. Its released software/assets are not an eligible drop-in for this repository's commercial-license and Mac constraints. No code or model assets have been copied. [Authors' project](https://shenhanqian.github.io/gaussian-avatars), [official code and license](https://github.com/ShenhanQian/GaussianAvatars).

**Astra** accepts image inputs and returns analysis/structured annotations. It is not an image-output model or a calibrated metrology tool. Here it proposes semantic observations; camera geometry and source-pixel checks constrain their use. Skin hidden by an opaque frame is still inferred. Raster cleanup uses the separate image-generation model, with explicit registration and a bounded composite; it is not an Astra image output. [Official model documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-astra).

**GNM**, Google's statistical head model, is a possible alternative identity prior with a permissive implementation. A local NumPy landmark-fit experiment used the already available assets, but did not establish better rendered geometry, rig compatibility or closed topology. It was not adopted into the production path. A sparse fitting score alone is insufficient evidence for changing the head representation. [Official repository](https://github.com/google/gnm), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23687).

**Multiview texturing** requires its own alignment and seam controls after geometry reconstruction. Waechter et al. describe view selection and global/local color adjustments; these address a different problem from Poisson surface reconstruction. This pass fixes demonstrated ownership and blending discontinuities in our implementation; it does not implement that paper's optimization or claim equivalent results. [Authors' paper](https://download.mmag.hrz.tu-darmstadt.de/media/FB20/GCC/paper/Waechter-2014-LTB.pdf).

## Next research stage, not yet implemented

The promising head-specific approach is a shared evidence model with separate ownership for skin, ears, eyeballs, eyelids, brows, beard, hair and eyewear. Jointly refine camera calibration, neutral surface, expressions and materials while explaining all selected frames. Each region should carry measured coverage and uncertainty. An accessory should occlude skin during fitting rather than becoming skin color. Hair should have a constrained outer volume plus surface-bound guide curves; dense strands add detail after that volume agrees with the photographs.

Use robust landmark, silhouette, multiview feature and photometric losses with smoothness, thickness and collision constraints. Estimate illumination/exposure separately from base color before offering relighting. Use learned models only to initialize or fill explicitly unobserved regions. Optimize the compact head representation locally using CPU/MPS-compatible operations; evaluate heavier hosted alternatives separately before making dependency or account commitments.

A benchmark should include several consenting identities, skin tones, hair types, glasses/no-glasses, both profiles, back and upward/downward views. Hold out entire camera angles and expressions. Compare silhouette error, landmark error, identity preservation, texture seams, accessory residue, hair boundary error, material response, rig stability and latency. Test calibrated synthetic cameras against their stored focal ground truth. Passing tests on one phone orbit does not establish generalization.

The current scan covers about 233 degrees, not a full orbit. Its unseen regions, glasses-hidden skin and insufficiently resolved eye detail cannot be recovered faithfully from missing evidence. “Works on any video” should mean robust handling and honest quality feedback; hyperrealistic identity recovery requires enough sharp, overlapping, well-lit observations. The current implementation still uses a generic facial/inner-ear prior and captured illumination, and has not reached the proposed joint inverse-rendering stage.

## Local use

```sh
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/rebuild_head_details.py .local/face-captures/CAPTURE_ID
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/prepare_hair_review.py .local/face-captures/CAPTURE_ID
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python -m unittest tests.head_semantics_test tests.head_template_test tests.hair_eyewear_test
```

Serial/threaded texture equivalence can be checked without publishing or changing the capture:

```sh
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/verify_texture_bake.py .local/face-captures/CAPTURE_ID --report .local/texture-verification.json
```

This runs one-worker and four-worker bakes against cached inputs, compares exact PNG bytes and atlas metadata, rejects source writes/network access, and checks that the relevant code did not change during the run.

Accepted model artifacts are resolved with `head_artifacts.published_folder(capture)`. New releases live under `CAPTURE/.model-generations/`; `model-release.json` identifies the current bundle. Root images and AI caches remain reusable inputs. Legacy root model files are retained for old sessions and are not the latest model after a new release. Use the current build/refinement commands above; the old Gaussian/legacy surface writers refuse to overwrite a generation-based capture.

The matched review runs at `/hair-review.html` on the development server. Inspect the actual source and rendered model together; a successful rebuild is not sufficient visual validation.
